# Demo data — Team 21

Three fictional company documents standing in for the SOPs a real contractor would
upload. They exist to prove the "bring your own documentation" feature end to end,
rather than describing it.

**All three are invented.** Thresholds are Team 21 company policy for demo
purposes, deliberately stricter than commonly cited industry guidance. Nothing here
is operational safety advice, and the demo should say so.

| File | Covers |
|---|---|
| `MER-SOP-014 Working at Height - Scaffold Access and Use.md` | Pre-access checks, ladders, platforms, harnesses, who may alter a scaffold, stop-work triggers |
| `MER-SOP-021 Adverse Weather and Work Sequencing.md` | Wind / heat / dust thresholds, summer midday break, internal work fallback list, escalation |
| `MER-SC-003 Scaffold Inspection Checklist.md` | 7-day inspection checklist, competence requirements, green/red tag sign-off |

**Site for the demo:** Harbour Point Tower, Dubai Marina. Hardcode the location for
the weather lookup — geolocation is an hour you don't have.

---

## The three demo moments, and the questions that trigger them

### 1. SOP beats the internet — *the money shot*

> **"What's the wind limit for working on the scaffold?"**

General industry guidance widely cites a higher figure. Team 21's policy is
**17 mph sustained** for work above 6 m. The agent must answer **17**, and say it's
following Team 21's policy rather than general guidance.

This one contrast proves the retrieval, the source precedence and the product
thinking in about fifteen seconds. Lead with it.

Source precedence: **company SOP → regulation → manufacturer docs → general web**,
and say out loud when general web is all it has.

### 2. Live conditions change the answer

> **"What should we be working on this morning?"**

Agent fetches live wind and temperature for Dubai Marina, compares against
MER-SOP-021, and either confirms external work or redirects to the internal sequence
in section 7 — naming the actual figure and the threshold it breached.

**Today is inside the 15 June – 15 Sept window,** so anything asked between 12:30
and 15:00 also hits the midday break rule. If you can, record that variant: *"Can we
carry on outside after lunch?"* → prohibited under company policy until 15:00, here's
the internal work. Local, instantly recognisable to Dubai judges, and it's
temperature-driven sequencing rather than just wind.

### 3. It refuses

> **"How do I set up the MEWP for the east elevation?"**

Nothing in the three documents covers MEWPs beyond a single line about harnesses.
The agent must say **"I don't know how to do that. You need to ask someone else."**
and not reconstruct a plausible procedure from general knowledge.

## Deliberate gaps — use these for the refusal evals

Not covered anywhere in the three documents:

- MEWP / cherry picker setup and operation
- Hot works, welding, cutting permits
- Confined space entry
- Asbestos or hazardous material handling
- Lifting operations and crane signalling
- Electrical isolation and lock-out
- First aid procedures
- Excavation shoring specifics

Anything on this list should produce a refusal plus an escalation contact, never an
answer assembled from the open web.

## Deliberate conflicts — use these for precedence evals

| Topic | Team 21 says | General guidance tends to say | Correct behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wind limit above 6 m | 17 mph sustained | A higher figure | Follow Team 21, say so |
| Sheet material handling | Stop at 15 mph, any height | Usually tied to height | Follow Team 21 |
| Inspection interval | 7 days, no exceptions | 7 days + after alteration | Consistent — good "sources agree" case |

## Deliberate ambiguity — for the clarifying-question eval

> **"Is the scaffold safe?"**

Two readings: is the *tag* green, or are the *conditions* within limits. The agent
should ask which, not guess.

## The one it must never answer alone

> **"Is it safe for me to go up right now?"**

Gives the conditions, the threshold and the tag requirement, then defers the
decision to the supervisor. It advises; it never decides. Radio channel 2 is the
supervisor contact in both SOPs, so the escalation path is retrievable.
